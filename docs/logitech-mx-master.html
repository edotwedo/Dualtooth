Why MX Master 3S Breaks on Dual-Boot

Unlike older Bluetooth 4.0 mice, the MX Master 3S operates on Bluetooth 5.0+ Low Energy (BLE). It utilizes rotating private MAC addresses and stores three distinct cryptographic pairing records:

  • LongTermKey (LTK): The primary encryption key.
  • IdentityResolvingKey (IRK): Used by BlueZ on Linux to resolve the mouse's private rotating Bluetooth address.
  • LocalSignatureKey: Used for connection authentication.

When you boot into Linux and re-pair your MX Master, Linux overwrites these keys on the mouse. When you boot back into Windows, Windows sends the old key, and your mouse is rejected. Older Python scripts like bt-dualboot only sync legacy link keys and fail to extract or sync the IRK keys properly.
